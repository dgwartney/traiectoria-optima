# Airlines Information

The primary U.S. carriers dominating operations at the nation's major airports are American Airlines, Delta Air Lines, United Airlines, and Southwest Airlines. Together, they operate out of virtually all large hub airports. [1, 2, 3] 
A total of 10 primary mainline airlines fly out of major U.S. airports. Their alignment with the country's largest transit hubs breaks down into distinct operational tiers. [4, 5] 

## The Global Network Carriers
These airlines operate massive hub-and-spoke networks, utilizing major international airports to connect domestic routes with global destinations. [4] 
 
- American Airlines: Operates its largest hubs out of Dallas/Fort Worth (DFW), Charlotte (CLT), Chicago O'Hare (ORD), and Miami (MIA). It maintains a heavy presence at competitive coastal entry points like Los Angeles (LAX) and New York (JFK). [4, 6, 7] 
- Delta Air Lines: Anchors its network at Hartsfield-Jackson Atlanta International Airport (ATL)—the world's busiest airport. It runs large hub operations out of Minneapolis/St. Paul (MSP), Detroit (DTW), Salt Lake City (SLC), Seattle (SEA), and New York (LGA/JFK). [4, 6, 8, 9] 
- United Airlines: Routes its massive domestic traffic through critical hub airports including Chicago O'Hare (ORD), Denver (DEN), Houston Intercontinental (IAH), Newark (EWR), and San Francisco (SFO). [4] 
* 

## The Major Low-Cost & Point-to-Point Carriers
These airlines focus heavily on dense domestic markets, linking large metropolitan regions and vacation destinations. [4, 10, 11] 

- Southwest Airlines: Unlike legacy hub carriers, Southwest relies on large "focus cities" with immense traffic, dominating airports like Dallas Love Field (DAL), Chicago Midway (MDW), Baltimore/Washington (BWI), Las Vegas (LAS), and Denver (DEN). [4] 
- JetBlue Airways: Commands a major footprint at coastal gateways, primarily operating out of New York (JFK), Boston (BOS), Fort Lauderdale (FLL), and Los Angeles (LAX). [4] 
- Alaska Airlines: Controls the Pacific Northwest and West Coast markets through major hubs at Seattle-Tacoma (SEA), Portland (PDX), San Francisco (SFO), and Los Angeles (LAX). Following its merger, its portfolio includes Hawaiian Airlines, which manages traffic through Honolulu (HNL). [4, 8] 

## The Ultra-Low-Cost Carriers (ULCCs)
These value-focused lines connect major vacation centers and secondary metro airports, though they frequently share gates at major international hubs. [4, 12] 

- Frontier Airlines: Heavily serves Denver (DEN), Orlando (MCO), Las Vegas (LAS), and Atlanta (ATL).
- Spirit Airlines: Operates high-volume routes out of large hubs like Fort Lauderdale (FLL), Orlando (MCO), Las Vegas (LAS), and Detroit (DTW).
- Sun Country Airlines: Functions primarily out of its main hub at Minneapolis/St. Paul (MSP).
- Allegiant Air: Focuses on connecting smaller regional towns to major leisure destinations like Las Vegas (LAS), Orlando/Sanford (SFB), and Phoenix/Mesa (AZA). [4, 13, 14, 15]
